Heat Warning Agent

It phones outdoor workers when heat crosses a dangerous limit, and confirms they understood. Not that they answered. That they understood.

A worker who says “haan” has not understood anything

Karachi’s 2015 heatwave killed over a thousand people in a week, most of them working outdoors. The forecasts existed. They never arrived.

The obvious fix is to phone people. The non-obvious problem is that reflexive agreement to an authoritative voice on a phone is the default behaviour. A naive flow records that yes as a successful warning, and the employer ends up holding a compliance record full of agreement that meant nothing, which is worse than holding no record at all.

So this agent never asks a question that “yes” can answer. It asks what you are going to do, and where you are going to sit.

What it records

  • Understood the warning. Only true if they said something showing comprehension. Their own words are kept as evidence beside the judgement, so a reviewer can check the call rather than trust it.
  • Stopped work. And if they cannot, why. “I’m paid per delivery” is the most useful thing a call can learn, and no thermometer can tell you it.
  • Has water and shade.
  • Distress interrupt. Dizziness, nausea, confusion or having stopped sweating abandons the script immediately and escalates to a human.

Every fact starts null, meaning not established, which is a different state from false. A call that drops before the water question must never record that the worker had no water.

Urdu, honestly

AssemblyAI streams 18 languages with native code-switching. Urdu is not among them. Hindi is.

Spoken Urdu and spoken Hindi are the same language. They diverge in script and in formal register, not in “I stopped, I have water.” Using the Hindi model for Urdu speakers is an approximation, it is documented as one, and it needs testing against real Karachi speech before anyone relies on it. The code-switching matters too, because Karachi workers mix Urdu and English constantly.
